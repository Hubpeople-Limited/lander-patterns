The pricing page built around a real table, with every plan a column and every
thing a visitor might care about a row. Instead of three cards each repeating a
list, two plans can be read against each other line by line without scrolling
back and forth.

It suits a brand whose plans differ on a dozen small details - how many messages,
which filters, whether profiles can be hidden - where a card would either become
a wall of text or leave the differences out. It is also the page to choose when
support is regularly asked what the difference actually is.

It does not flatter a simple offer. Two plans differing on one number look
thinner in a table than they do as cards, and a table of four rows invites the
question of why it is a table at all.
