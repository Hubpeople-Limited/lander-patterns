```recipe
recipe: features-compared@1
shape: comparison
shell: features-table
grounds: deep, plain, soft, brand
opens: decide it from the material - the first screen promises a straight comparison: the table's own first rows can be it, or a claim on the dark ground above them
closes: decide it from what the table has settled - a band after the questions, a single link, or the last question
signature: straight into the table, questions
pairing: brand
notes: the table is the page - for somebody deciding between plans, not browsing
```

The features page for a visitor who is comparing rather than exploring. The
first screen says plainly that the page is going to lay the plans out - the
table itself can be that screen - and the table is the page: every plan a
column, every thing a visitor might care about a row, the recommended column
named in words. The questions follow the table, because they are the questions
a table raises.

It suits a brand whose free and paid tiers are close enough that the difference
needs stating carefully, and one confident enough to state it. Nothing stands
between the first screen and the table: a visitor who already knows what the
site does is not walked through it again.

It does not welcome anybody, and it does not tour the product. Opening on a
comparison promise is the wrong first impression for a visitor arriving from
an advertisement, who should land on a homepage instead; a visitor who does not
yet know what the site does wants the panels of `features-what-you-get` before
any table.
