```recipe
look: hero-stated alignment=centred
grounds: deep, plain, soft, brand

closes: a full-width band in the brand colour, after the questions
signature: dark centred opener, straight into the table, questions, band
pairing: brand
notes: the table is the page - for somebody deciding between plans, not browsing
```

The features page for a visitor who is comparing rather than exploring. The dark
centred opener says plainly that the page is going to lay the plans out, and the
table comes straight after it: every plan a column, every thing a visitor might
care about a row, the recommended column named in words. The questions follow
the table, because they are the questions a table raises.

It suits a brand whose free and paid tiers are close enough that the difference
needs stating carefully, and one confident enough to state it. Nothing stands
between the opener and the table: a visitor who already knows what the site does
is not walked through it again.

It does not welcome anybody, and it does not tour the product. Opening on a dark
ground with a comparison promise is the wrong first impression for a visitor
arriving from an advertisement, who should land on a homepage instead; a visitor
who does not yet know what the site does wants the panels of
`features-what-you-get` before any table.
