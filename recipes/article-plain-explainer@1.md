The ordinary advice article: a bylined header, a summary a reader gets without
reading on, the piece itself, where its figures came from, and who wrote it. The
takeaways box sits near the top and gives the answer to anybody who only wants
the answer.

It suits any piece of editorial between about six hundred and fifteen hundred
words, and it is the shape to use unless there is a reason to use another. The
takeaways box does most of the work: most people who arrive from a search read it
and leave, and the page should still have served them.

It does not carry a long piece. Past about fifteen hundred words a reader needs
somewhere to navigate from, and this page has no contents list. It also expects
real sources - the line under the writing is worse than useless if there is
nothing genuine to put in it.
